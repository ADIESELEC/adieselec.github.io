5 Most Common Electrical Code Violations in Cape Town Homes (And How to Fix Them)

Living in the Cape Town Metro means enjoying beautiful views and a great lifestyle, but hidden dangers can sometimes lurk inside older homes. Whether you are renovating a house in Retreat or managing a rental property across the city, understanding electrical code violations is vital for safety and legality. In South Africa, these standards are set out in the SANS 10142-1 wiring code. Ignoring them can lead to fire, injury, or failed insurance claims.

Here is what you need to know about the violations inspectors most often find, and who to call to put things right. At Adie's Electrical Solutions, we frequently encounter these exact issues in Cape Town homes, and knowing the signs can save you time, money, and stress.

Why Electrical Compliance Matters in South Africa

It isn't just about following rules; it is about protecting your future. An electrical certificate of compliance (CoC) is a legal document that proves your installation meets safety standards. Without it, you cannot legally transfer property ownership in most cases. Insurance companies may refuse to pay for fire damage if an old or faulty installation is found to be the cause. For landlords, an out-of-date installation can create serious liability. Ensuring your home meets the current national standard protects your family, your wallet, and your legal standing.

The 5 Most Frequent Wiring Violations Found Locally

Over the years, we have seen a recurring pattern of issues that put families at risk. If you notice any of these signs during your own safety check, it is time to call a professional. Below are the most common offenders found in Cape Town properties.

  • Unlicensed or Poor Quality DIY Work: Home improvement projects often involve plugging in lights or adding sockets. When this is done by handymen without proper training, connections can loosen or cables can be wrong for the load, creating a fire hazard.
  • Missing Safety Switches (RCDs): Residual Current Devices, known as safety switches or RCDs, cut off power instantly if current leaks. Older homes were built before these were mandatory in all circuits. Missing RCDs leave occupants vulnerable to severe electric shock.
  • Inadequate or Missing Earthing and Bonding: Earthing directs stray electricity safely into the ground. Bonding connects metal parts like pipes and bath taps. Without this, a fault in an appliance can make the casing live, which is extremely dangerous.
  • Overloaded Circuits and Wrong-Sized Cables: Modern homes use more appliances than ever. Running air conditioners, braai stoves, and geysers on old, thin cables causes overheating. If fuses blow frequently or sockets get hot to the touch, you likely have an overload issue.
  • Outdated or Non-Existent Certificate of Compliance: Many homes on the market lack a valid CoC. When a new owner moves in with an old certificate, the electrical system is not guaranteed safe under current laws. This is a common stumbling block when buying or selling.

Even if the wiring still works, one violation can invalidate the safety of the whole system. A thorough inspection is the only way to know for sure.

A Special Note for Cape Town Landlords

If you rent out property in the Metro, your responsibility is higher. Landlords must ensure the electrical installation is safe and compliant for tenants. A valid CoC is often required before a tenant can safely occupy the property, and failure to provide a safe environment exposes you to legal claims if an accident occurs. Regular maintenance and prompt repairs are not just good practice; they are a requirement of duty of care.

How to Fix These Violations

Getting compliant is a clear process. First, you need a qualified electrician to inspect the installation and identify what fails against the standard. Once you have a written report, you can prioritize repairs. Some issues, like adding RCDs, are relatively quick to fix. Others, like rewiring an old house, take more time and planning. Do not attempt to patch over problems yourself; only a registered practitioner can certify the work once it is done.

Understanding the Cost of Compliance

It is natural to worry about the budget. Costs depend heavily on the size of the property, its age, and how much rectification is needed. Minor repairs and upgrades can range from a few hundred to a few thousand rand, while a full rewire of an older home is a significant investment. Because every property is unique, final pricing depends on an on-site assessment, so a quick conversation about your needs is the best way to start.
